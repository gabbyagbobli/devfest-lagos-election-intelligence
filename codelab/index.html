1. Codelab Overview & What We Are Building

Duration: 3:00

Welcome to the DevFest Lagos 2026 hands-on workshop: Building a Trustworthy AI Agent for Election Intelligence with ADK and the Antigravity Harness.

In this 40-minute hands-on codelab, you will build, orchestrate, test, and run a multi-agent Election Intelligence application powered by Google's Agent Development Kit (ADK 2) and engineered inside the Antigravity Harness.

The Problem: Election Misinformation vs. Grounded Intelligence

As elections approach, citizens, journalists, and civic observers increasingly rely on the internet and AI systems for information about candidates, electoral procedures, PVC collection deadlines, BVAS accreditation rules, official announcements, and breaking developments. Unfortunately, fabricated claims, doctored postponement notices, outdated guidelines, and unverified viral messages make it difficult to separate fact from speculation.

Instead of building an ordinary chatbot that generates plausible-sounding answers from its static training memory, you will build an agentic AI system that retrieves authoritative records, calls deterministic verification tools, coordinates specialized agents, and communicates uncertainty transparently.

What You Will Build

Your finished application demonstrates eight core trustworthy AI capabilities:

  1. Election Information Retrieval from an allowlisted corpus of official electoral regulations and statutory provisions.
  2. Retrieval & Grounding Using Reliable Sources via a 3-Tier Electoral Source Trust Registry (Tier 1 Statutory/INEC, Tier 2 Accredited Observers & Fact-Checkers, Tier 3 Verified News Media).
  3. Verification of Claims Against Evidence with explicit epistemic verdicts (SUPPORTED, CONTRADICTED, or INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE).
  4. Summarization of Official Election Announcements and statutory timetables.
  5. Comparison of Information from Multiple Credible Sources across official, observer, and media perspectives.
  6. Strict Source Attribution & References with document IDs (e.g., [INEC-2026-PROC-01]), publication dates, and official URLs.
  7. Transparent Communication of Uncertainty when evidence is incomplete, conflicting, or unverified.
  8. Multi-Agent Collaboration & Orchestration using Google ADK (Agent, FunctionTool, sub_agents, and Runner).
💡 Core Architectural Principle — Retrieval ≠ Verification:
Finding a statement online does not automatically make it true. Throughout this workshop, your application will distinguish between the credibility of a source, the evidence it provides, and the conclusion that can reasonably be drawn.

Why This Step Matters

In Google ADK, any Python function wrapped in FunctionTool(fn) becomes callable by the LLM. The model reads the function's name, type annotations, and docstring to understand when to invoke it and what arguments to pass. By returning explicit doc_id, source_tier, trust_score, published_date, and url metadata from our tool, we force the agent to ground its answers in verifiable records.

def search_verified_election_corpus(query: str, category: str = "all") -> dict[str, Any]:
    """
    Searches the verified election intelligence corpus for official guidelines,
    electoral commission announcements, statutory laws, and accredited observer reports.

    Args:
        query: The natural language search query or topic keywords (e.g., "voting procedures BVAS PVC", "election date postponement").
        category: Optional category filter. One of "all", "voting_procedures", "official_announcement", "fact_check", "legal_framework", "multi_source_development", or "unverified_investigation".

    Returns:
        A dictionary containing matched documents with full source attribution,
        trust tier scores, publication dates, URLs, and key facts.
    """
    corpus = load_election_corpus()
    registry = load_trusted_sources()
    query_tokens = _tokenize(query)
    q_lower = query.lower()

    scored_docs: list[tuple[float, dict[str, Any]]] = []
    for doc in corpus:
        if category and category != "all" and doc.get("category") != category:
            continue
        haystack = " ".join([
            doc.get("title", ""),
            doc.get("summary", ""),
            doc.get("content", ""),
            " ".join(doc.get("topics", [])),
            " ".join(doc.get("key_facts", [])),
        ]).lower()

        score = 0.0
        for token in query_tokens:
            if token in haystack:
                score += 2.0
            if any(token in topic.lower() for topic in doc.get("topics", [])):
                score += 3.0
            if token in doc.get("title", "").lower():
                score += 2.5

        for topic in doc.get("topics", []):
            if topic.lower() in q_lower:
                score += 4.0

        if score > 0:
            tier_meta = _get_tier_metadata(doc.get("source_tier", ""), registry)
            enriched = {
                "doc_id": doc["doc_id"],
                "title": doc["title"],
                "source_name": doc["source_name"],
                "source_tier": doc["source_tier"],
                "tier_label": tier_meta["tier_label"],
                "trust_score": tier_meta["trust_score"],
                "published_date": doc["published_date"],
                "url": doc["url"],
                "category": doc["category"],
                "summary": doc["summary"],
                "content": doc["content"],
                "key_facts": doc.get("key_facts", []),
                "relevance_score": round(score, 2),
            }
            scored_docs.append((score + tier_meta["trust_score"], enriched))

    scored_docs.sort(key=lambda item: item[0], reverse=True)
    top_docs = [doc for _, doc in scored_docs[:5]]

    if not top_docs:
        return {
            "status": "no_verified_match",
            "query": query,
            "match_count": 0,
            "documents": [],
            "epistemic_notice": "No matching records were found in the verified election corpus. Do NOT invent facts or citations.",
        }

    return {
        "status": "success",
        "query": query,
        "match_count": len(top_docs),
        "documents": top_docs,
    }

def check_source_credibility(source_or_url: str) -> dict[str, Any]:
    """
    Evaluates a domain, URL, or organization name against the Three-Tier Electoral
    Source Trust Registry to determine its authority and verification rules.
    """
    registry = load_trusted_sources()
    candidate = source_or_url.strip().lower()
    parsed = urlparse(candidate if "://" in candidate else f"https://{candidate}")
    hostname = (parsed.hostname or candidate).lower().removeprefix("www.")

    for tier_key, tier_data in registry.get("tiers", {}).items():
        for src in tier_data.get("sources", []):
            domains = [d.lower().removeprefix("www.") for d in src.get("domains", [])]
            if hostname in domains or any(hostname.endswith(f".{d}") for d in domains):
                return {
                    "status": "allowlisted",
                    "source_id": src["source_id"],
                    "source_name": src["name"],
                    "tier_key": tier_key,
                    "tier_label": tier_data["label"],
                    "trust_score": tier_data["trust_score"],
                    "verification_rule": tier_data["verification_rule"],
                    "is_trusted": True,
                }
    return {
        "status": "unverified_source",
        "tier_key": "UNVERIFIED",
        "trust_score": 0.0,
        "is_trusted": False,
    }

2. **Fact-Checking Rumors, Viral Claims & "Can you verify..." Queries** -> Delegate to `claim_verification_agent` (or use `verify_election_claim`).
3. **Summarizing Official Announcements or Comparing Multiple Credible Sources** -> Delegate to `election_information_analyst` (or use `get_official_announcements` / `compare_multi_source_coverage`).

### Mandatory Trust & Epistemic Guardrails
- **Retrieval != Verification**: Always distinguish between the authority of a source (Tier 1 Statutory/INEC, Tier 2 Accredited Observer/Fact-Checker, Tier 3 Verified Media) and the conclusion drawn.
- **Explicit Verification Status**: State clearly whether a claim or topic is `SUPPORTED`, `CONTRADICTED`, `INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE`, or `OFFICIAL_GUIDANCE`.
- **Strict Source Attribution**: Cite every claim using `[doc_id]` (e.g., `[INEC-2026-PROC-01]`, `[YIAGA-2026-OBS-01]`), the source name, publication date, and official URL.
- **Transparent Uncertainty**: When evidence is incomplete, conflicting, or unverified (such as the Eti-Osa/Alimosho polling unit relocation rumors), explicitly state what is known, what is unverified, and why no definitive conclusion can be drawn.
- **Zero Fabrication**: Never invent election results, voting procedures, candidate statements, or URLs.
"""

Key Architectural Takeaways

  1. Multi-Agent Separation of Responsibilities: Splitting coordination (election_intelligence_coordinator), grounded retrieval (election_research_agent), evidentiary claim verification (claim_verification_agent), and multi-source synthesis (election_information_analyst) produces modular, auditable agent behavior.
  2. Retrieval ≠ Verification: Finding a keyword match in a corpus or search engine does not make a claim true. Trustworthy agents evaluate source tier authority, corroboration across independent observers, and statutory compliance.
  3. Epistemic Humility by Design: In high-stakes domains like elections, healthcare, finance, and law, an AI system must clearly state when a claim is INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE rather than guessing or fabricating citations.
